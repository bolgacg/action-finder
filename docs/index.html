Before anything else

Five things to know

This is the instrument, not the argument. Here is what it does and what it refuses to do.

  1. Science Bridge starts Actions: small interdisciplinary collaborations where researchers and external partners explore one specific challenge together. Finding the pairs worth starting one about is the job this page does a piece of.
  2. A candidate here is one department at the Faculty of Natural Sciences paired with one research topic. It never proposes a person.
  3. A pair qualifies when the department publishes in that topic, Danish organisations are working on the same topic in European projects, and the two have not already met.
  4. Every candidate carries the evidence that produced it: the papers, the organisations, the counts, and the reason it was scored the way it was. You can disagree with the score and recompute it.
  5. The last section is the one that matters most. It says what this instrument cannot tell you, using numbers it computed about itself.

What the job actually asks for

The advertisement describes the work in a way that names its hardest part, and that part is what this page attacks.

Initiating, structuring and driving Actions, smaller, interdisciplinary collaborations where researchers and external partners share knowledge and jointly explore specific challenges [...] Facilitating dialogue and matchmaking between researchers and relevant companies, organisations and public authorities. Aarhus University, Science Bridge, Innovation specialist, position 165085, read 2 September 2026

Matchmaking at a faculty that publishes tens of thousands of papers is a search problem before it is a relationship problem. A specialist who already knows a field can name the pairs from memory; a new secretariat covering eight departments cannot, and that is exactly where an instrument helps. The rest of the job, the part this page does not touch, is everything that happens after the introduction.

Five things it is not

  1. Not a capability match. A shared topic label means two groups write about similar things. It does not mean one can do what the other needs.
  2. Not a topic label you should trust without reading it. The Danish side takes its topic from the keywords a project carries in CORDIS, and some of those keywords are homonyms used loosely.
  3. Not a complete picture of Danish industry. The partner side comes from European project participation, which is the part that is open and joinable. The Danish company register is behind a signed declaration and has no bulk download, and since the industry codes changed on 1 January 2025 they can no longer separate a biotechnology firm from any other natural-science research company.
  4. Not evidence that nobody has met. Absence of a shared project and of a company co-author is absence in two public records, not in the world.
  5. Not a recommendation about a person. The unit is a department. Named researchers appear only where they already appear publicly, as authors of the papers cited as evidence, and only when you ask for them.

Handling of personal data

The Pure feeds carry institutional email addresses, usernames and employee identifiers next to author names. All three are stripped at harvest, before anything is written to disk, and the code says why at the point it does it. Nothing person-level is computed, ranked or stored, and the files this page ships contain no identifier from which a person could be ranked. Author names appear only behind the control in act one, only for papers that are already public, and only as the citation they already are.

The Aarhus University Pure portal reserves text and data mining rights in its footer, which is a reservation under Article 4(3) of the EU copyright directive, so the portal itself is not scraped. The harvest uses the OAI-PMH endpoint, which exists to be harvested and feeds the Danish national research database, with a self-identifying user agent and spaced requests. Before anything built from it were published more widely, the right step is a note to the Pure administrators, who sit in the same administration as Science Bridge.

Limits, stated before you find them

The score is a dull formula and is exposed next to every row it produced. It is there to put the list in an order, not to be believed. Two reasonable people would weight it differently and the page hands them what they need to do that.
